Wear OS in the simulator

A Wear OS APK is detected from its manifest (a required android.hardware.type.watch feature) and runs on a round 454×454 watch display. Configuration.isScreenRound() and the watch UI mode read as they do on a watch, rotary sends the crown’s scroll events, and screenshots are cropped to the circle. --launcher adds a Wear system UI around the app: a watch face, the app launcher, the notification stream, the tiles and widgets carousel, and quick settings.

compose-preview android-simulator wear.apk --launcher                 # the app, over a watch face
compose-preview android-simulator wear.apk --tile .MyTileService      # a tile in the carousel
compose-preview android-simulator --watchface face.apk                # a WFF watch face, nothing else
compose-preview android-simulator --watchface face.apk --install comp.apk   # face + complication app
compose-preview android-simulator phone.apk --pair wear.apk           # a phone and its watch
compose-preview android-simulator wear.apk --data-layer agent --control stdio  # the agent as the phone
On a watch Goes to Command
Back from the app’s root, or Home the watch face home
The side button the launcher key STEM_PRIMARY, launcher
Swipe up on the face the notification stream notifications {"op":"open"}

Tiles and Wear Widgets

The carousel holds what you add, with --tile <service> (repeatable), tiles {"op":"add"}, or its own add page.

  • Tiles are bound by BIND_TILE_PROVIDER and called over their real AIDL: tile and resource requests, and the add / enter / leave / remove callbacks. They’re drawn by the protolayout tile renderer, which the CLI provides (apps don’t ship it). A LoadAction requests the tile again with the clicked state, and a LaunchAction starts its activity.
  • Wear Widgets (Glance Wear) are bound by BIND_WIDGET_PROVIDER, asked for a Remote Compose document at their container’s size, and played with the Remote Compose player. They’re shown the way a watch shows them: just the provider’s preferred container — small (2×1) or large (2×2), a stadium on a round watch and a squircle on a square one — with no card or label.

Both read the watch’s simulated system data. health sets heart rate, steps, distance, calories and floors, which tiles read through PlatformHealthSources and widgets through Glance Wear’s HealthData.

→ {"cmd":"tiles","op":"add","component":".GoalsTileService"}
→ {"cmd":"health","heartRate":72,"steps":5400,"stepGoal":8000}
→ {"cmd":"screenshot","path":"tile.png"}

Watch faces

--watchface <apk | package | component> (or watchface {"op":"set"}) replaces the demo face with one of two kinds:

  • AndroidX watch faces (WatchFaceService, ListenableWatchFaceService) are bound through the library’s WatchFaceControlService and drawn headless, interactive or ambient. This is the same path Wear’s face editor and Android Studio’s previews use.
  • Declarative Watch Faces in the Watch Face Format (WFF, watch_face_info.xml) run one of two ways:
    • Google’s runtime. When DeclarativeWatchFaceRuntime.apk is cached, the simulator hosts the WFF package in Google’s own runtime. The APK is never redistributed: compose-preview android-simulator dwf-runtime pull --from-adb copies it from your own Wear emulator, and dwf-runtime install <apk> registers a local copy.
    • The built-in compiler. Without the runtime (or with --dwf-runtime none), the face’s XML is compiled to Remote Compose and played in-process.

    Either way, [STEP_PERCENT], [BATTERY_PERCENT], [HEART_RATE], [WEATHER.*], [ACCELEROMETER_ANGLE_*], [CONFIGURATION.*] and [COMPLICATION.*] follow the simulated watch state.

The clock, health, battery, weather and accel commands set that state, and ambient, tilt and palm move the screen between interactive, ambient and off. watchface {"op":"config"} sets user styles and ColorConfiguration options. Flavors switch too.

Watch Face Push. An app calling WatchFacePushManager’s addWatchFace, updateWatchFace, listWatchFaces, removeWatchFace or setWatchFaceAsActive gets a simulated Watch Face Push service. The agent can push faces too, with watchface {"op":"push"}. Pushed WFF APKs are installed and activated live.

Dynamic theme colors. By default (--theme-from-watchface), the active face’s accent palette becomes the system’s Material 3 dynamic colors, so Wear apps, tiles and Glance widgets that use dynamicColorScheme(context) follow the face.

Complications

watchface {"op":"complication"} fills a slot from a ComplicationDataSourceService in one of these places:

  • in the same APK as the face;
  • in another APK installed beside it with --install <comp.apk>. The ComplicationData crosses between the two simulator JVMs as real parcels.

Without a component, the command fills the slot with an inline fake complication (type, text, title, value, min, max), so you can test a face’s slots without writing a data source. Timelines resolve against the simulated clock, and watchface {"op":"tap"} fires a complication’s tapAction.

→ {"cmd":"watchface","op":"complication","slot":0,"component":"com.example.comp/.StepsDataSource"}
→ {"cmd":"watchface","op":"complication","slot":1,"type":"RANGED_VALUE","value":62,"min":0,"max":100,"text":"62%"}
→ {"cmd":"watchface","op":"tap","slot":0}

For repeatable watch face checks, --scenario replays clock, health, battery, weather, ambient and tap steps headlessly. It asserts on the frame (changedFrom, litPixelRatio, regionColor) and on the watch face’s status, then writes report.json and exits 2 on failure.

Notifications, ongoing activities and ambient

  • The notification stream. Notifications are drawn the way Wear draws them from their extras: title, text, MessagingStyle, big picture, and WearableExtender actions and reply choices. A high-importance post peeks over the face.
  • Ongoing activities (androidx.wear.ongoing) appear as the face’s chip and as the launcher’s first rows, with their timers on the simulated clock.
  • Ambient mode. AmbientLifecycleObserver, the legacy AmbientModeSupport, Horologist’s AmbientAware and Wear Compose’s rememberAmbientModeManager() all get onEnterAmbient, onUpdateAmbient and onExitAmbient. An app that doesn’t support ambient is stopped and restarted as it would be on a watch.

Data Layer pairing

--pair <watch.apk> runs the watch app in a second simulator and connects the two apps with a simulated Wearable Data Layer. DataClient, MessageClient, NodeClient, CapabilityClient and ChannelClient answer from the simulator instead of Play services. Messages, requests, data items, assets, capabilities and channel streams cross between the apps and reach their live listeners and manifest WearableListenerServices.

Each side is also a CompanionDeviceManager association. These work across the pair too:

  • RemoteActivityHelper, including its availability status;
  • Horologist’s DataLayerAppHelper;
  • on the watch: RemoteAuthClient, BridgingManager and PhoneTypeHelper.

With --control stdio, "device":"watch" sends a command to the watch, every line says which device it came from, and each crossing is a datalayer event.

The agent as the peer. --data-layer agent needs no second app: the control channel plays the other device. Everything the app sends arrives as a datalayer send event, decoded as text or a DataMap when it can be. The agent answers with dataLayer actions: connect, disconnect, capabilities, put / delete, message / request, reply, and the channel operations. You can test a watch app’s sync, error and disconnected-phone paths without building the phone app.

→ {"cmd":"dataLayer","action":"connect","capabilities":["verify_remote_app"]}
← {"event":"datalayer","kind":"send","type":"message","path":"/start","requestId":4,"text":"…"}
→ {"cmd":"dataLayer","action":"reply","requestId":4,"text":"ok"}
→ {"cmd":"dataLayer","action":"put","path":"/settings","dataMap":{"units":"metric"}}

Wear gaps

Area Gap
Watch faces AndroidX GlesRenderer faces don’t run (Robolectric has no EGL); CanvasRenderer faces do. The on-watch face editor UI isn’t drawn: watchface {"op":"config"} sets options directly.
Declarative faces Running Google’s own runtime needs your copy of DeclarativeWatchFaceRuntime.apk, pulled from a Wear emulator. The built-in compiler approximates the runtime, so it isn’t pixel-identical. scripts/dwf-diff.py renders a face both ways side by side when that matters.
Tiles A tile’s TileUpdateRequester isn’t wired to the carousel: tiles {"op":"refresh"} requests it again.
Ambient Ambient reaches the app, but nothing enforces it. The system doesn’t dim, shift for burn-in or cap the frame rate. Ambient offload and auto-resume are accepted and ignored, and AmbientTransitionListener isn’t called.
Paired phone The phone’s bridged notifications and launcher entries aren’t shown on the watch. Both apps are always running, so delivery that starts a stopped app isn’t exercised.
Data Layer fidelity getCapability leaves out the local node, FILTER_REACHABLE is ignored, and onPeerConnected isn’t delivered. ChannelClient has no flow control. Listeners always run on the main thread.
Data Layer APIs The deprecated GoogleApiClient APIs (Wearable.DataApi, MessageApi, …) aren’t simulated. R8-minified builds rename the clients the simulator matches by name, so use a debug build.
Wear SDK Only RemoteInteractionsManager and Watch Face Push are simulated: Sdk.getWearManager returns null for any other service.
Without pairing With neither --pair nor --data-layer agent, the Wearable clients report API_UNAVAILABLE.
